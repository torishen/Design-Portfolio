01 — Background & Pain Points

The gap between playing guitar and using it.

Most guitar learning apps treat the instrument in isolation — chords, tabs, strumming patterns. But the moment a learner tries to sing along or play with a vocalist, they hit a wall that the app was never designed to help them through.

This feature was designed for two specific users: the beginner guitarist who is also learning to sing, and the guitarist who wants to play alongside a vocalist in a band setting. Both share the same core problem — there is no bridge between learning guitar and using it in a musical context with a voice.

"I never know if I'm even in the right key when I try to sing along."
User Type 1

Beginner guitarist learning to sing

Has no pitch reference. Doesn't know if their voice is in the right key. Feels anxious about singing and playing at the same time. Needs scaffolding and clear feedback.

User Type 2

Guitarist playing with a vocalist

Needs to transpose songs to match their singer's range quickly. Currently does this manually by ear or gives up entirely. Needs pitch-sorted tools and fast context-switching.

02 — UX Research

Listening before designing.

Research began with a contextual inquiry — observing and interviewing guitar learners in their natural practice environments. Participants were watched as they used existing guitar apps, tried to sing along, and attempted to coordinate with other musicians. Key moments of confusion and frustration were documented and grouped using affinity diagramming.

Four major themes emerged from the affinity clusters, each becoming a direct design constraint for the feature's architecture.

F-01
Navigation complexity. Learners struggled to move fluidly between chord view, practice mode, and vocal setup. Each extra screen increased drop-off risk.
F-02
Pitch reference gap. Beginners had no way to know if they were singing in the right key. They wanted audio confirmation, not just visual indicators.
F-03
Tabs as primary content. Across all users, tabs and chord diagrams were the most-used elements. Any vocal feature had to support — not compete with — that content.
F-04
Lack of closure feedback. Users finished flows without knowing what had changed or what to do next. Completion felt ambiguous rather than rewarding.
03 — Design Inspirations

Theory meeting interface.

Learning Science · Ch. 4

Expert Blind Spot

What feels obvious to an experienced musician is genuinely opaque to a beginner. The pitch detection screen was given step-by-step instructions because experts routinely skip steps they've forgotten were ever hard.

Scaffolding
Learning Science · Ch. 2

Form Fits Function

Tabs automatically update when a vocal track is applied so the information reorganizes itself around the learner's actual task, rather than forcing a mental translation between two different keys.

Knowledge Structure
Mayer's Principles

Multimedia Learning

Chord diagrams, written tabs, and an audio vocal reference track appear on the same screen. Multiple formats for the same content reinforce each other and deepen retention.

Multimodal
Nielsen Heuristics · H2-1

Visibility of System Status

An "Updated" pill on the tabs section and a full summary screen after applying a vocal track ensure users always know what changed and that the system responded to their action.

Feedback
Nielsen Heuristics · H2-6

Recognition Over Recall

The pitch legend (+/−/~) is shown in the track library before the list appears. Users see what badges mean rather than needing to remember it from a tooltip they read two screens ago.

Usability
Lecture 18 · Color

Grayscale First

The mid-fi prototype was designed entirely in grayscale to establish hierarchy through luminance and spacing before color is introduced — ensuring the design works structurally before it works visually.

05 — User Testing & Final Prototype

What five users taught me.

Five users tested the mid-fidelity prototype in a contextual inquiry format — using it as they would during a real practice session. A consistent theme emerged: the feature was useful once understood, but the path to understanding it was unclear.

Person 1

The feature was a bit difficult to navigate, but the addition of chord charts was extremely helpful for a guitar learner.

Person 2

The emphasis on voice adjustment could be reduced — more focus should go to the tabs, which are the main content during playing.

Person 3

Liked the UI, but needed help understanding what to do right after recording their voice.

Person 4

The interface design and flow were effective together — improvement for feedback detail would be helpful in the final screen.

Person 5

The voice feature is helpful, but needs further explanation as to why it's needed. More useful for guitar learners with specific needs.

The clearest theme: users found the feature valuable once they understood it — but the path to understanding was the problem.

What I changed

01
Added purpose framing at entry. A short explanation at the top of the vocal flow tells users why pitch matching matters before they commit to the setup — addressing the transfer problem raised by Person 5.
02
Tabs made dominant. Restructured the song screen so chord and tab content leads visually, with the vocal track as a clearly supporting element — directly responding to Person 2.
03
Post-recording guidance improved. The screen after recording now clearly directs users to "Choose Existing Vocal Track" as their next step — fixing the drop-off point Person 3 identified.
04
Applied Summary screen added. A new confirmation screen surfaces the applied key, pitch adjustment, tab update status, and newly unlocked practice mode — giving Person 4 the closure they needed.
